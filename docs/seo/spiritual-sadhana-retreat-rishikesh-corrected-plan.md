## 1. Executive Decision

The Gemini plan is directionally useful, but it should not be implemented exactly as written.

Use the structure from Gemini, but correct these points before implementation:

- Avoid broad or unverified claims such as guaranteed transformation, authentic Himalayan lineage, zero hidden fees, or clean Himalayan air unless the site can prove them.
- Keep the language simpler. The current page already reads serious and grounded; the rewrite must improve search targeting without making readability harder.
- Use `spiritual sadhana retreat in Rishikesh` as the best page-specific target, while still capturing `spiritual retreat in Rishikesh`.
- Do not force noisy keywords from the CSV. Ignore irrelevant autocomplete terms such as Canada border, house for sale, Kannada vlogs, flood, and similar non-buyer terms.
- Add images because the current live page has no modality-specific images. This weakens SXO, image SEO, and trust.
- Keep strong safety boundaries because spiritual pages can easily drift into overpromising, guru-dependency language, or mental-health risk.

## 7. Educational Guide Plan

### Section Title

`What to understand before choosing spiritual sadhana`

### Article Blocks

1. `What spiritual sadhana means`
   - Define sadhana clearly.
   - Mention meditation, breath, mantra, silence, study, reflection, seva and conscious daily choices.
   - Keep it practical and non-sectarian.

2. `Why people explore a spiritual retreat`
   - Address fatigue, transition, meaning, identity, grief, purpose and inner discipline.
   - Avoid saying the retreat cures these issues.

3. `Why Rishikesh matters`
   - Include `why is Rishikesh sacred` and `what is special about Rishikesh`.
   - Phrase carefully: Rishikesh is widely known as a major place for yoga, meditation and Ganga-side contemplative practice.
   - Avoid historical claims unless sourced.

4. `How it works inside a retreat`
   - Explain daily rhythm, repetition and integration.
   - Mention that every guest does not need the same intensity.

5. `How Shreevan approaches it`
   - Consent, context, choice, non-coercive practice.
   - No forced belief or disclosure.

6. `What beginners should know`
   - Beginners can start with short practice.
   - Sadhana is steadiness, not complexity.

7. `What to be careful about`
   - Spiritual bypassing.
   - Guru dependency.
   - Using retreat to avoid clinical care or practical responsibilities.
